# Journal submission editorial assessment — October 2026

## Scope and decision

The current manuscript is a descriptive, reproducibility-focused paper combining three **non-causally linked** evidence streams: (i) preserved synthetic AI calculation responses, (ii) researcher-proposed partial O*NET task-statement correspondences, and (iii) 2021–2025 descriptive OEWS metropolitan employment and real wage comparisons.

**Proposed first journal to examine:** *AI & SOCIETY* (Springer Nature), a hybrid journal welcoming reflective and contextual analysis of the social, cultural, economic, ethical and policy dimensions of AI. Official journal overview: https://link.springer.com/journal/146 . This is a conditional fit, not an acceptance prediction.

**Alternative:** *PLOS ONE*, which evaluates original research with emphasis on scientific and methodological soundness. The paper will need a clear original empirical or methodological contribution and a rigorous reporting design. Source: https://journals.plos.org/plosone/s/criteria-for-publication . The PLOS publication-fees page currently lists $2,477 for standard PLOS ONE articles and describes fee assistance: https://plos.org/fees/ . Verify fees when submitting.

## Major issues to resolve before any journal submission

1. **Core scientific contribution is diffuse.** Benchmark answers, O*NET mapping, and OEWS changes answer different questions. Organize around an explicit *evidence-boundary/reproducibility* research question, or select one evidence stream as the main study and treat others as contextual, not inferentially combined.
2. **Benchmarks have incomplete first-attempt provenance.** Report only agreement for saved response files (26/26; 140/140 fields) and describe prior identity corrections. Do not estimate model first-attempt success or professional-task automation.
3. **O*NET source veracity is not mapping validity.** Three original O*NET statements were directly source-verified, but no independent expert has validated the proposed AI mappings; do not infer exposure or occupational automation.
4. **Labor sample selection and external validity.** Ten occupations × 212 common county-stable metros × five years are not nationally representative. Explain the occupation-selection rule and justify why they form an informative sample. Distinguish 46-metro/20-occupation historical analyses from the 266–282 occupation-specific endpoint sensitivity.
5. **OEWS measurement limitations.** Survey-panel overlap, source-metro delineation and NAICS changes, suppression and reporting limitations need a coherent limitations section. Source-to-processed reconciliation 50/50 employment and 50/50 wages is complete as a *researcher-executed* run, but this is not external replication.
6. **Statistical/qualitative contribution.** If the manuscript remains descriptive, explicitly justify why these three streams are integrated in a single original paper. Do not add regression, uncertainty bands, p-values, peer reviews or new observations without actual documented analyses.
7. **Figures and literature.** Review the final five original figures one by one, confirm units, labels and data-source captions, distinguish source-verified and conceptual literature, ensure every in-text citation matches a full reference.
8. **Submission disclosures.** Author Peter Sarpong; independent researcher, Gaithersburg, Maryland, USA; no external funding; no competing interests; transparent generative-AI assistance declaration. Check chosen journal's AI policy. The manuscript is separate from the Zenodo versioned *research materials*.
9. **Persistent archive.** Continue to cite existing Zenodo DOI https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23437477 (GitHub v3.4.7) without creating a new version solely for manuscript revisions. Do not claim post-v3.4.7 GitHub commits are included in that DOI snapshot.

## Recommended structure (AI & SOCIETY route)

- Title foregrounding **limits of inference**, avoiding claims about measured AI impacts on employment.
- Introduction: the problem of conflating technical ability, task exposure, adoption, and labor outcomes.
- Conceptual framework: distinct evidence levels and inferential requirements.
- Evidence and transparent methods: saved-case audit, task-source crosswalk, OEWS metropolitan descriptive panel and sensitivity.
- Results: short, non-causal presentation with appropriately labeled tables.
- Discussion: what can and cannot be inferred; policy and responsible communication.
- Limitations: provenance, small benchmark scope, selected occupations, BLS construction, O*NET time mismatch.
- Conclusion: methodological contribution, not an asserted causal AI employment finding.
- Declarations, availability (Zenodo DOI), references and figure captions.
